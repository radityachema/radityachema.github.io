On 17 November 2026, I presented our paper *Co-Simulation of Vehicle-to-Building (V2B) and Thermal Loads: A Scalable Testbed for Integrated Demand Response in Commercial Facilities* at **ASim 2026**, held at the National University of Singapore (16–18 November). It was my first conference talk as an NTU PhD student.

The testbed separates the physical world (road network, ride demand, EV fleet, buildings) from the decision-maker, so a heuristic, an optimisation model or a learned policy can be swapped in without touching the simulator. The code is open source: [radityachema/v2b-cosim](https://github.com/radityachema/v2b-cosim), and the tag [`asim-2026`](https://github.com/radityachema/v2b-cosim/tree/asim-2026) is the exact version behind the paper's results.

The question from the floor was about heterogeneous vehicles. The simulator already takes per-vehicle battery capacity, power limits and state-of-charge bounds; the scenarios in the paper use a homogeneous fleet, and mixed fleets are a natural next step.
